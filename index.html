BATCH: Behavior Alignment for Cross-Embodiment Learning from Humans

Why Behavior Alignment?

Human demonstrations provide a scalable source of manipulation data, but they are not simply additional data from a different visual domain. As the two videos below illustrate, the human completes the task much faster than the robot and includes an extra segment to reset the cup that has no counterpart in the robot trajectory. Prior co-training approaches typically sample human and robot trajectories independently, so paired clips may correspond to different stages of the task—for example, the human may be grasping the cup while the robot is placing it on saucer. BATCH instead performs behavior alignment, identifying frame-level correspondences between equivalent stages of task execution across embodiments.

Method

BATCH has two parts. (A) Behavior alignment learns a shared temporal embedding from unaligned human and robot demonstrations and uses DTW to retrieve behavior-aligned segment pairs. (B) A cross-embodiment video action model trains on these pairs with joint co-training and human-to-robot style transfer.
